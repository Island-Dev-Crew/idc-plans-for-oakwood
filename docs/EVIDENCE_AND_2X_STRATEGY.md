# IDC Plans for Oakwood — Evidence, Confidence Grade, and 2× Program Architecture

**Assessment date:** August 21, 2026
**Prepared by:** Island Development Crew
**Comparison rule:** grade each program against the layer it actually claims. AAMU is a university degree/research ecosystem; IDC’s Oakwood work is an implementation blueprint and portfolio-production operating model. They are complementary categories, not interchangeable products.

## Executive finding

AAMU moved first on institutionalization. Its 2026–2027 bulletin now publishes a **125-credit B.S. in Artificial Intelligence**, supported by an existing AI concentration, an AI Cage with Sandia National Laboratories, a Center for AI/ML & Cybersecurity, a university AI policy, and 2026 designation as one of five AWS–Machine Learning University regional leads. That is a real academic and partnership moat.

IDC’s Oakwood body of work is stronger where many degree programs are weakest: converting infrastructure into visible student output. The Oakwood plan combines a verified-room 10-seat local-AI studio, edge/robotics, fabrication, security, responsible-AI formation, Huntsville employer demos, and an eight-product portfolio promise. The Campus Companion enhancement is a concrete early proof that the operating model can compress idea-to-deployment time.

The right answer is not to imitate AAMU course-for-course. Oakwood should build a **smaller, faster AI Production & Proof program** that sits on top of an academically rigorous CS foundation and makes every course terminate in a verified body of work.

## What “2× better” means here

It does **not** mean twice the computers or twice the claims. It means doubling the conversion rate from coursework to trusted opportunity:

1. **Every learning unit creates evidence.** No invisible assignments. Repositories, eval reports, model cards, user tests, demos, and reflections are captured.
2. **Every artifact has a trust trail.** Human contribution, AI contribution, data rights, testing, accessibility, security, and real-user impact are declared.
3. **Every semester crosses disciplines.** CS + business + health + education + theology + media + agriculture/community problems.
4. **Every student can run AI three ways.** Frontier cloud, open-weight local, and edge/physical AI.
5. **Every lab zone maps to curriculum and a public outcome.** Hardware cannot become an expensive display.
6. **Every graduate leaves with an employer-readable proof ledger.** Credentials supplement, not replace, the transcript.

## The 2× Oakwood model: AI Production & Proof Institute

### Lane 1 — Foundations (credit-bearing academic rigor)

- Python, data structures, algorithms, databases, systems, networks
- Calculus, linear algebra, probability/statistics
- Machine learning, deep learning, NLP, vision, reinforcement learning
- Human-computer interaction and accessibility
- Security, privacy, AI policy, model governance
- Two-semester production capstone

### Lane 2 — Studio (ship every term)

1. **AI-Augmented Builder** — computer-use agents, software engineering, tests, deployment
2. **Knowledge Systems** — RAG, retrieval evaluation, document intelligence, institutional search
3. **Local Model Operations** — quantization, inference serving, throughput/latency, cost and energy
4. **Multimodal Media Studio** — image/audio/video generation, provenance, accessibility
5. **Physical & Edge AI** — robotics, sensors, Jetson-class deployment, 3D-printed enclosures
6. **AI Security Lab** — prompt injection, data leakage, model/system red-team, incident response
7. **Applied Research Studio** — reproducibility, dataset cards, experiments, paper/poster
8. **Venture & Community Deployment** — customer discovery, procurement, maintenance, measurable impact

### Lane 3 — Proof (the differentiator)

Each artifact receives a 100-point **Body-of-Work Confidence Score**:

| Signal | Points | Required evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Shipped artifact | 25 | Live URL/binary, tagged release, working demo |
| Evidence completeness | 20 | Repository, tests, architecture record, model/data card, provenance |
| Reliability & safety | 15 | Eval suite, accessibility check, threat model, failure modes |
| AI fluency | 15 | Model/tool choices, prompt or agent traces where safe, local/cloud trade-off |
| User or research impact | 15 | User test, benchmark, adoption, experiment result, or community outcome |
| Collaboration & reflection | 10 | Contribution record, mentor review, lessons, next iteration |

**Anti-gaming rule:** a polished interface without code/testing/provenance cannot exceed 55. A strong credential without a live or reproducible artifact cannot exceed 60. AI-generated volume does not raise the score unless verification quality rises with it.

### LinkedIn/employer portability

LinkedIn should be treated as a destination, not the source of truth. The proof ledger generates:

- a public credential page suitable for LinkedIn’s Featured section;
- an Open Badges/Verifiable Credential-compatible record;
- concise skill statements backed by artifact URLs;
- a recruiter view showing verified competencies, not self-declared keyword lists;
- a downloadable evidence packet for employers that cannot access private repositories.

No claim is made that LinkedIn currently supports a custom ranking algorithm or direct candidate-scoring API. Integration would use supported profile/credential surfaces unless a formal partner API is obtained.

## Campus Companion as the first Proof Ledger case

The original Campus Companion was a meaningful student initiative. IDC’s enhanced version is useful as a validator because it exposes the difference between **having an idea** and **closing the production loop**:

- broader building and department coverage;
- historical/contextual content;
- responsive multi-device UX;
- accessibility attributes and keyboard support;
- loading/error states and WebGL fallback;
- feedback capture and deployment evidence;
- documented iteration history.

The next version should turn the comparison into a reusable rubric: artifact quality, evidence completeness, accessibility, reliability, user value, collaboration, and AI-method disclosure. The lesson is not “AI replaced twelve students.” The lesson is “AI mentorship and an evidence-gated delivery system can increase what a student team is able to finish, verify, and show.”

## 10-seat lab architecture — revised August 2026

### Seat and zone pattern

- **6 maker seats:** high-memory unified-memory or GPU workstations for development/local inference
- **2 specialist seats:** one media/3D workstation and one security/evaluation workstation
- **1 faculty/research console:** orchestration, classroom control, reproducibility archive
- **1 adaptive/demo seat:** accessible workstation + employer/project demonstrations
- **Shared local inference node(s):** vendor-neutral procurement; size by memory, support, throughput and curriculum, not hype
- **Edge bench:** robotics/sensors/embedded AI
- **Fabrication bench:** soldering, fume extraction, 3D printing/CNC where facility controls permit
- **Proof wall:** live portfolio/evaluation dashboard rather than marketing slides
- **Evidence vault:** NAS/object storage with role-based access, backup, retention, and dataset/model provenance

### Procurement gates

1. Licensed electrical load study and FPE-panel decision before energizing new compute.
2. Campus IT approval for switching, identity, VLANs, logging, and remote management.
3. Cooling/noise/egress/accessibility review.
4. Three sealed quotes per trade and clean separation of grant-eligible equipment from facility work.
5. Hardware bake-off using actual Oakwood workloads before scaling a single architecture.
6. Annual sustainment owner, software budget, spares, and student-lab-assistant plan signed before purchase.

## 12-month launch sequence

- **0–30 days:** governance charter, room validation, workload bake-off, curriculum council, employer advisory circle.
- **31–90 days:** facility work, network/security baseline, equipment commissioning, faculty/staff training, first five proof-ledger templates.
- **Semester 1:** 10–15 student pilot; Campus Companion as reference artifact; four shipped products; public demo/review.
- **Semester 2:** cross-disciplinary studio; local-model and physical-AI work; employer reviews; Open Badge/credential pilot.
- **Year-end:** publish an outcomes report with artifact count, verified scores, accessibility/security defects closed, internship interviews, user adoption, research submissions, and cost per verified artifact.
