# Refero Popular 100 — Pattern Study

**Captured:** August 21, 2026
**Source:** https://styles.refero.design/ sorted by **Popular**
**Evidence:** `refero-popular-100.json` + `refero-popular-slow-scroll.webm`

## Method

1. Opened Refero Styles and activated the Popular view.
2. Recorded a slow browser scroll for visual evidence.
3. Verified the public same-site endpoint `https://styles.refero.design/api/styles?page=N&sort=popular`.
4. Preserved pages 1–5: 20 records each, 100 sequential unique style URLs.
5. Preserved the first 100 titles/descriptions and source links.
6. Selected patterns by transferability to an HBCU institutional strategy hub—not by trendiness alone.

Refero does not publish the popularity formula or scoring period, so the ordering is captured accurately but its methodology cannot be independently validated.

## Rejected patterns

- **Neon cyberpunk / violet glass:** too generic for institutional credibility and too detached from Oakwood’s heritage.
- **Liquid iridescence:** visually fashionable but weak for evidence-heavy governance content.
- **Playful mascot systems:** wrong authority level for chair, cabinet, faculty and funder audiences.
- **Pure white Apple minimalism:** elegant but underpowered for the volume of proof, comparison and implementation detail.
- **Brutalist agency styling:** creates novelty at the cost of legibility and trust.

## Chosen treatment

**Heritage Signal / Research Ledger**

- Oakwood navy as the institutional field.
- Old gold as a decision signal, not decoration.
- Warm ivory as archive/paper.
- Restrained AAMU maroon only inside comparison bars.
- Large editorial serif thesis statements.
- Mono evidence labels and chapter indices.
- No gradient hero, no generic glass cards, no floating cyber-AI blobs.
- Motion limited to progress, interaction, entrance, and state change.

## Evidence limitation

Refero’s Popular ordering is a live product state and can change. The recording and JSON preserve what was visible during this build; they are design-study evidence, not a permanent canonical ranking.
